009                                              A BSTRACT
010
011
               Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent sys-
012            tems, where effective inter-model communication is crucial. Existing communi-
013            cation protocols either rely on natural language, incurring high inference costs
014            and information loss, or on hidden states, which suffer from information concen-
015            tration bias and inefficiency. To address these limitations, we propose KVComm,
016            a novel communication framework that enables efficient communication between
017            LLMs through selective sharing of KV pairs. KVComm leverages the rich infor-
018            mation encoded in the KV pairs while avoiding the pitfalls of hidden states. We
019            introduce a KV layer-wise selection strategy based on attention importance scores
020
               with a Gaussian prior to identify the most informative KV pairs for communica-
               tion. Extensive experiments across diverse tasks and model pairs demonstrate that
021
               KVComm achieves comparable performance to the upper-bound method, which
022
               directly merges inputs to one model without any communication, while transmit-
023            ting as few as 30% of layers’ KV pairs. Our study highlights the potential of KV
024            pairs as an effective medium for inter-LLM communication, paving the way for
025            scalable and efficient multi-agent systems.

      1   I NTRODUCTION
030
031   Large Language Models (LLMs) have catalyzed a paradigm shift from isolated model capabilities
032   towards collaborative multi-agent systems (Guo et al., 2024; Tran et al., 2025). CAMEL (Li et al.,
033
      2023), AutoGen (Wu et al., 2024), and ChatDev (Qian et al., 2023) have demonstrated the potential
      of LLMs to collaborate effectively in multi-agent systems, achieving impressive results in various
034
      tasks. These systems leverage the strengths of individual LLMs and enable them to work together
035
      to solve complex problems that are beyond the capabilities of a single model (Yang et al., 2024a).
036
037   While multi-agent systems have shown great promise, they also introduce new challenges, particu-
038   larly in the area of inter-agent communication. Effective communication between LLMs is crucial
039
      for the success of multi-agent systems. Explicit communication through natural language has been
      explored in several works, enabling the models to share information (Du et al., 2023), coordinate
040
      their actions (Sun et al., 2025), and make collective decisions (Yang et al., 2024b).
041
042   However, natural language communication leads to high inference costs due to the need for multi-
043   ple decoding steps, and may not fully capture the rich information that needs to be shared between
044   LLMs as information is lost in the sampling process (Pham et al., 2023; Ramesh & Li, 2025) that
045
      occurs as each new token is produced. To address this limitation, recent works have explored alter-
      native communication protocols that leverage the internal representations of LLMs. CIPHER (Pham
046
      et al., 2023) proposed to use the embedding space as the medium of communication between LLMs.
047
      Namely, they pass the weighted average of the token embeddings from one LLM to another, facilitat-
048   ing more efficient information exchange. Rather than using the embedding space, AC (Ramesh & Li,
049   2025) transmits the intermediate activations, specifically the last token’s hidden state. They replace
050   the last token’s hidden state of the receiver’s model (Mr ) with that of the sender’s model (Ms ),
051   allowing a more direct transfer of information. While these methods have shown promising results,
052   they still face challenges in terms of communication efficiency and effectiveness. CIPHER (Pham
053   et al., 2023) still requires multiple decoding steps, which can be costly, and AC (Ramesh & Li, 2025)
      may lead to information loss as only limited activation information is transmitted.

      We start with the question: What is the most effective way to communicate between LLMs? We
      argue that an ideal communication protocol should satisfy the following criteria: ① Effectiveness: It
070
      should enable Mr to effectively utilize the information from Ms . ② Efficiency: It should minimize
071
      the computation needed by Ms and the amount of data transmitted between models. ③ Generality:
072   It should be applicable to a wide range of tasks and model architectures, ensuring its versatility in
073   different scenarios. We choose to use activation information as the medium of communication, as
074   no decoding steps are needed for Ms , and Mr can directly utilize the rich information encoded in
075   the activations. We study different types of activation information (i.e., hidden states and KV pairs),
076   and in Section 2.2, we show that hidden states suffer from information concentration bias, where
077   the last token’s hidden state contains most of the information needed for the model’s output. This
078   makes it challenging to design an effective communication protocol using the last token’s hidden
079   state. Furthermore, we find that using all tokens’ hidden states from a single layer of the sender Ms
080
      does not guarantee effective communication.R4-Q3 A dilemma arises: if the hidden states are taken
      from the early layers of Ms , the computation benefit is limited since the computation cost is similar
081
      to concatenating the two inputs; if the hidden states are prepended to the later layers of Mr , the
082
      performance drops significantly.
083
084   Based on these observations, we propose KVComm, a novel communication protocol that enables
085   efficient communication between LLMs through selective sharing of KV pairs. KV pairs are the
086
      most representative activation information in each layer, and sharing them does not interact with
      the hidden states of Mr directly, while Mr can decide how to utilize the information through the
087
      attention mechanism. To further improve the efficiency of communication, we propose a selection
088
      strategy to choose which (potentially non-contiguous) layers’ KV pairs to share. We formulate
089   hypotheses that (H1) KV pairs from intermediate layers encode transferable semantic knowledge,
090   and (H2) KV pairs from layers exhibiting stronger attention distributions are more effective for
091   communication. These hypotheses are validated by our experiments in Sections 4.3 and 4.5. Based
092   on these hypotheses, we define attention importance scores for each layer based on the average
093   attention weights assigned to the context tokens. We also apply a Gaussian distribution centered
094   at a certain layer as a prior on the attention importance scores. The intuition is that the Gaussian
095   distribution encourages selecting layers around a certain depth, which aligns with hypothesis H1.
096   The general framework is illustrated in Figure 1.
097   We evaluate KVComm on a diverse set of tasks with eight model pairs (see Section 4.1), showing
098   that it consistently outperforms existing communication protocols while significantly reducing the
099   data transmitted between models. In summary, our work makes three key contributions:
100
101     • We evaluate different types of activation information for communication between LLMs, and
102       identify the limitations of using hidden states as the medium of communication. We show
103
          that the last token’s hidden state suffers from information concentration bias, and point out a
          dilemma that arises when using all tokens’ hidden states.
104
105     • We propose KVComm, a novel communication protocol that enables efficient communication
106       between LLMs through selective sharing of KV pairs. We design a selection strategy based on
107       attention importance scores and a Gaussian prior to choose which layers’ KV pairs to share.
          This is the first approach that makes it possible to choose non-contiguous layers of KV. More-

109         for a given pair of models, prior to deployment.

      2.1     P ROBLEM F ORMULATION
123
124   We formally define the problem of solving a contextual task through the communication of two
125   LLMs: Ms and Mr . Ms takes as input a context C, and generates the required information IC to
126   be communicated. Mr takes as input the query Q and the information IC from Ms , and produces
127   the final output. In this work, we limit the choices of the two LLMs to (1) two instances of the
128   same LLM, and (2) two models that are fine-tuned versions of the same base LLM. The objective
129   is to design a communication protocol which jointly optimizes the communication, computation
130   efficiency, and the information fidelity between Ms and Mr R3-Q1 .
131
132   2.2     W HY H IDDEN S TATES FALL S HORT
133
134   When Decoder-Only LLMs infer, the input information flows through the model in the form of
135
      activation values, which refer to the intermediate results output by each decoder layer during the
      forward pass. We refer to the intermediate activation values that are passed between adjacent layers
136
      as hidden states. We also consider the KV pairs used in the attention mechanism within each layer
137
      as another type of activation information. In this section, we investigate the effectiveness of using
138   hidden states as the medium of communication by studying two questions: How important are
139   hidden states of tokens at different positions in the sequence? (Section 2.2.1) Are hidden states of
140   all tokens effective for communication? (Section 2.2.2)
141
142   2.2.1    T OKEN I MPORTANCE AT D IFFERENT P OSITIONS
143
144   We begin with a simple experiment examining how token positions affect performance. Using
145   Llama-3.1-8B on MMLU Social Science, we remove or retain the hidden state of only specific
146
      tokens at a given layer and measure the performance change. As shown in Figure 2, different to-
      kens vary in importance across layers, with the last token becoming most critical in later layers.
147
      This aligns with the intuition that the last token is often the most relevant to the current prediction.
148
      Thus, the last token’s hidden state carries the most influential information for both model output and
149   inter-LLM communication. Results on additional datasets and models are provided in Appendix C.
150
151   To ensure efficient communication with hidden states built on this observation, two conditions must
152
      hold: (1) Ms must send at least the last token’s hidden state, and (2) the communication protocol
      should preserve Mr ’s last token state as much as possible. The protocol in Ramesh & Li (2025)
153
      either replaces Mr ’s last token state with that of Ms or averages the two, but both cause information
154
      loss in Mr ’s last token state, harming its performance.
155

161   effective if the hidden states are taken from the early layers of Ms and prepended to the early layers

183     3              E FFICIENT LLM C OMMUNICATION THROUGH S ELECTIVE KV S HARING
184
185    We propose a simple yet effective communication protocol that enables efficient communication
186    between LLMs by selectively sharing KV pairs. This approach addresses the limitations observed in
187    previous methods by ensuring that the most critical information is preserved. Our design satisfies the
188    three criteria outlined below: it enhances effectiveness by allowing Mr to utilize essential context
189
       (①), improves efficiency by reducing unnecessary computation and transmission overhead (②), and
       ensures generality by being applicable across diverse tasks and architectures (③).
190
191
192     3.1            C OMMUNICATION F RAMEWORK
193
       For a given context C and query Q, Ms processes the context C and runs one forward pass (prefill
194
       stage) to generate the KV pairs {(kls , vsl )} at each layer l, where l = 1, 2, . . . , L and L is the total
195    number of layers in Ms . We apply a selection strategy to choose a subset of KV pairs {(klsi , vsli )},
196    where i = 1, 2, . . . , M and M is the number of selected layers. The selected KV pairs are then
197    transmitted to Mr .
198
       Mr processes the query Q and incorporates the received KV pairs during its forward passes (prefill
199
       and decoding stages). Specifically, at each layer l of Mr , if l corresponds to a selected layer li 1 ,
200
       the KV pairs from Ms are integrated into the attention mechanism. We simply concatenate the KV
201    pairs from Ms with those of Mr : klr ← [klsi ; klr ], and vrl ← [vsli ; vrl ]. This integration allows Mr
202    to attend to both its own context and the information provided by Ms . After processing the query
203    Q with the integrated KV pairs, Mr generates the final output.
204
205

208    pairs to transmit from Ms to Mr . Not all layers or attention heads contribute equally to encoding

224   The first hypothesis H1 is that KV pairs from intermediate layers contain the most readily trans-

242   intuition is that the Gaussian prior encourages selecting layers around a certain depth, which aligns
243   with hypothesis H1 that intermediate layers are more likely to contain transferable knowledge.

268
269   We analyze the computational complexity of our KVComm framework compared to baseline meth-
      ods. Compared to the NLD (Du et al., 2023) method, our method does not require multiple decoding

      steps for Ms , which significantly reduces the computation cost. When the number of tokens gen-
271   erated during debate (Du et al., 2023) is large, the computation margin of our method over NLD is
272   on the order of O(L(Ts + Tr + |Q|)2 d), where Ts and Tr are the number of tokens generated by
273   Ms and Mr in the debate, respectively, and |Q| and d are the number of tokens in the query and the

275   method also reduces the computation cost, especially when M is small. The computation margin of
276   our method over Skyline is on the order of O(|C|d(L(2|Q| + T ) − M (|Q| + T ))), where |C| is the
277   number of tokens in the context, and |T | is the number of tokens generated by Mr .

452
453     LLM Inference Acceleration Lots of work has focused on accelerating LLM inference.
454     Computation-level methods such as FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) and Memory-Efficient At-
455     tention (Rabe & Staats, 2021) reduce memory and speed up attention; system-level methods such
456     as vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) and DeepSpeed-Inference (Aminabadi et al., 2022) improve overall
457
        throughput and latency; and model-level methods such as quantization (Lin et al., 2024) and prun-
        ing (Ma et al., 2023) reduce model size and complexity. These works mainly focus on working with
458
        only one model processing a single long input with the aim of minimizing computation cost.R3-Q1
459
        These approaches are orthogonal to ours and can be combined with KVComm to further improve
460     efficiency.
461
462     Closest to our work are methods that reuse computation across decoding steps or requests. Gao
463
        et al. (2024) introduces a hierarchical KV caching system for all requests; Gim et al. (2024) reuses
        prompt KV caches across queries by decomposing inputs; Liu et al. (2024c) compresses KV caches
464
        into compact bitstreams; and Yao et al. (2025) combines multiple chunks’ KV caches by selectively
465
        recomputing a few tokens. In contrast, our work targets communication across different LLMs,
466     which is more challenging due to parameter differences. Moreover, while prior methods reuse KV
467     caches uniformly across layers, we enable selective sharing of KV caches from different layers,
468     further improving efficiency. We do not compare with these works since they are orthogonal to ours.
469
        DroidSpeak (Liu et al., 2024b) aims to accelerate inference for queries with shared prefixes. It
470
        reuses the partial KV cache of these prefixes among different queries. Specifically, it empirically
471
        selects a single contiguous chunk of layers and recomputes the rest with large calibration overhead,
472     whereas our strategy flexibly selects non-contiguous layers with low overhead, without needing to
473     recompute the remaining layers. Despite different problem settings, we compare their contiguous-
474     chunk strategy with ours in Section 4.3, showing the advantages of our approach.R1-Q2
475
        Ye et al. (2025) adjusts KV cache for shared content by referencing a pool of cached examples-
476
        termed anchors that store observed cache deviations under varying prefixes. Our work goes beyond
477
        this related work by: 1) enabling a different type of communication, where the receiver does not
478     have access to the context, 2) making it possible to efficiently and selectively choose layers of KV
479     pairs that will be transmitted, and 3) being able to work effectively across different models that are
480     fine-tuned from one model.R4-Q7
481
482     Inter-LLM Communication Communication between multiple LLMs has been explored in sev-
483     eral recent works. Most works focus on using natural language as the medium of communication.
484     For example, Du et al. (2023) proposed a natural language debate framework where LLMs itera-
485     tively critique each other’s answers in natural language to improve the final answer. Liang et al.
        (2023) followed a similar idea but introduced a judge model to manage the debate process.


                                                                               9
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2026




486
      CIPHER (Pham et al., 2023) proposed using embedding space as the medium of communication.
487   They pass the weighted average of the token embeddings from one LLM to another. Moreover,
488   AC (Ramesh & Li, 2025) proposed to use the last token’s hidden state as the medium of commu-
489   nication. They replace the last token’s hidden state of the receiver model with that of the sender
490   model. Instead, we propose to use the KV pairs as the medium, which can preserve more informa-
491   tion than just using the last token’s hidden state. We also propose a more effective selection strategy
492   for choosing which KV pairs to share, which can further improve efficiency.
493
494   KV Cache Optimization Several works have explored optimizing KV caches for a single LLM
495   by (1) compressing the KV caches to reduce memory usage (Ge et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a) or (2)
496   managing the KV caches (offloading) to improve the inference speed (Lee et al., 2024; Xiong et al.,
497
      2024). As our work focuses on layer-wise selection of KV caches for communication between two
      LLMs, these methods are orthogonal and can be combined with our method.

500   6   D ISCUSSION
501
502   In this section, we discuss the limitations, clarify the scope of current design choices, and outline
503   promising directions for future research. Additional discussions can be found in Appendix K.
504
505   Heterogeneous Model Architectures Our current KVComm framework assumes that both LLMs
506   share the same base architecture, i.e., identical models or fine-tuned versions of the same base LLM.
507
      This is because KV pair structures differ substantially across model families, making direct KV ex-
      change undefined. This architecture dependency is a practical limitation but not a fundamental one.
508
      Future work could explore learning latent projections, adapters, or other transformation functions to
509
      enable KV exchange across heterogeneous architectures.R1-Q1, R2-Q1, R3-Q4, R4-Q1
510
511   Multiple Sender/Receiver Extensions While we focus on a single sender-receiver pair in this
512   work, KVComm can be naturally extended to multiple senders and/or receivers. KVComm can
513   integrate information from multiple senders by concatenating KV caches, and multiple receivers
514   can independently select layers based on their own attention patterns. As shown in Appendix J, we
515   mathematically extend our framework to multiple senders, and perform a preliminary experiment
516   with two senders and one receiver, showing that multiple senders can improve performance due to
517   diversified information sources. However, a systematic study of scaling behaviors in larger multi-
518   agent networks remains future work.R4-Q1
519
520   Context-adaptive Online Calibration KVComm currently adopts a fixed layer-selection strat-
521
      egy after calibration for simplicity and computational efficiency, while context-adaptive selection is
      a promising extension. KVComm can naturally support online and dynamic selection. A demon-
522
      stration and analysis of this mechanism is provided in Appendix L.R2-Q2
523
524
      Layer Selection Priors Given our goal of keeping the method simple, efficient, and broadly repro-
525   ducible, we opt for the Gaussian prior. Other alternatives, such as entropy-weighted or data-driven,
526   are promising but introduce significantly higher complexity, e.g., larger calibration sets, training a
527   selector, or risking overfitting to a particular task distribution. Exploring more sophisticated priors
528   is an interesting direction for future work.R2-Q3

      7   C ONCLUSION
531
532
      In this work, we identified the potential of using KV pairs as an effective medium for communication
533
      between two LLMs. We proposed a novel KVComm framework that enables efficient communica-
534   tion by selectively sharing KV pairs between LLM models. We designed a selection strategy based
535   on attention importance scores and a Gaussian prior to select the most relevant layers. Extensive
536   experiments on diverse datasets and model pairs demonstrated that KVComm can achieve compa-
537   rable or even superior performance to the Skyline upper bound and other methods, while reducing
538   communication costs by up to 3x. We highlight the generalization ability of our selection strategy,
539   which can be effectively calibrated with only a single sample. Our work opens up new possibilities
      for efficient inter-LLM communication and paves the way for future research in this direction.

776   B.4       C OMPARED M ETHOD D ESCRIPTIONS
777
778
      We compare our proposed KVComm framework with the following methods:
779
                 • Baseline: Mr processes the query Q without any communication from Ms .
780
781              • Skyline: Mr directly processes the concatenation of the context C and query Q. This
782
                   serves as an upper bound for performance.
783              • Natural Language Debate (NLD) (Du et al., 2023): Each model generates an initial an-
784                swer, and then they iteratively critique each other’s answers in natural language for a fixed
785                number of rounds. Finally, one model produces the final answer based on the entire debate
786                history. Compared to the original debate style setting, we use an information-transfer style,
787
                   which explicitly prompts Ms that it has to summarize the context C in its initial answer.
                   We set the number of debate rounds to 2.R3-Q3
788
789              • CIPHER (Pham et al., 2023): Similar to NLD, but instead of communicating in natural
790                language, the models communicate by passing the weighted average of the token embed-
791
                   dings from one LLM to another. We use the same prompt as NLD, and set the number of
                   debate rounds to 2.
792
793              • AC (Ramesh & Li, 2025): Communicate with the last token’s hidden state. Replace the last
794                token’s hidden state of Mr with that of Ms . We also test with mean and sum operations.

1171   We have additional discussions on the details and choices of our method.
1172
1173
       Positional Embedding Coherence KVComm is designed to preserve positional coherence across all
       layers. For the receiver model, in each layer, we shift all its positions by |C|, where |C| is the length of the
1174   context. For selected layers, we concatenate the KV pairs of the sender at positions [0, |C|), and the KV pairs
1175   of the receiver follow at positions [|C|, |C| + |Q|). For non-selected layers, positions [0, |C|) are left empty
1176   (unattended), but the KV of the receiver still starts at position |C|. This approach ensures that all layers share a
1177   consistent positional frame, so the attention mechanism sees the same offsets at every depth, avoiding positional
1178
       drift across layers. We perform an ablation study to validate this design in Appendix M.R2-Q4
1179
       Communication Cost Under the scenario where agents are connected with high-bandwidth links, the com-
1180   munication cost is relatively low compared to recomputation cost (Jin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024c). KVComm
1181   is more preferred when the information exchange volume is large (e.g., long contexts) and the communication
1182   bandwidth is sufficient. In scenarios with limited bandwidth, further compression of KV pairs or more aggres-
1183   sive layer selection may be necessary, which we leave for future work.R3-Q1

1302   We compare the computational complexity of our KVComm framework with the Skyline method and the NLD
1303   method. Recall that L is the total number of layers in the model, M is the number of selected layers for
1304   communication. We use d to denote the hidden dimension of the model, and |Q| and |C| to denote the number
1305
       of tokens in the query and context, respectively. Suppose Mr would generate T tokens in total, and the number
       of generated tokens is the same across different methods. For NLD, Ms and Mr would each generate Ts and
1306   Tr tokens for the initial answer, respectively.
1307
1308
       Ignoring the embedding, output layers, and other minor components, the computational complexity of prefilling
       a sequence of length N with a single decoder layer is O(N d2 + N 2 d), while the complexity of decoding a
1309   single token is O(d2 +(N +i)d), where i is the index of the generated token. Therefore, the total computational
1310   complexity of Ms to process the context C is O(L(|C|d2 + |C|2 d)).
1311

1312   the context C, which is O(L(|C|d2 + |C|2 d)), (2) the complexity of Mr to process the query Q with the
1313   selected M KV pairs from Ms , which is O(L|Q|d2 + M (|C| + |Q|)|Q|d + (L − M )|Q|2 d), and (3) the
1314   complexity of Mr to generate T tokens with the selected M KV pairs from Ms , which is O(T (Ld2 +
1315   M (|C| + |Q| + T )d + (L − M )(|Q| + T )d)). Therefore, the total computational complexity of KVComm is:
